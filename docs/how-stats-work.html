How the stats work

Every number on your card is computed from your real, recent rated games — no vibes. Here's exactly what each one measures and how it's built.

TWO LEVELS OF DEPTH Your card first shows quick stats: your rating sets the number, and your playing style tilts it up or down. Because matchmaking pins almost everyone near a 50% win rate, we never let style-only signals decide strength — rating anchors it, so a world #1 always out-scores a strong amateur. Hit Deep analysis and Stockfish runs in your browser over your recent games to replace Attack, Defense, Endgame and Control with real, engine-measured values (below).

Overall

RATING

Your Overall starts from your rating percentile — not the raw number. Being 1500 means something different on Lichess than on Chess.com, so the card asks the same question on both: what fraction of players are you stronger than? That percentile is mapped onto a 40–99 card scale.

The compression problem — and the fix Percentiles flatten at the very top: a 2500 and a 2900 both read as "roughly 99th percentile," which is why a strong streamer and a world #1 used to tie at 96. So above the elite line the card switches to absolute rating to spread the top back out — 2700 ≠ 2800 ≠ 2900. World #1s land 98–99; very strong titled players sit a notch below.

A title (GM, IM, FM…) is permanent proof of strength that fast ratings understate, so titled players get a rating floor: an equal-rated amateur will never out-card a titled player.

Honest caveat: Chess.com doesn't publish percentiles, so those curves are approximate. Overall blends your rating percentile (heavily) with your six sub-stats (lightly).

Speed

CLOCK

How well you handle the clock — flagging, time scrambles, and bullet strength.

How it's computed Anchored to your bullet-rating percentile, then adjusted by your flag rate — the share of your losses that ended out of time rather than on the board. Rarely flag → Speed climbs. Lose on time a lot → it drops.

Needs at least ~5 losses to measure flagging directly; otherwise it's set from your bullet rating.

♛

Attack

AGGRESSION

How decisively you win — checkmates and converting winning games.

Quick stat Your rating, tilted by how decisively you win (checkmate rate and win rate).
With Deep analysis Stockfish looks for sound sacrifices — moves where you give up material but the engine says the position held or improved — and how quickly you convert winning positions. A weak player's "sacrifices" mostly just lose, so they don't count; that's why attack still tracks strength.
♜

Defense

RESILIENCE

How you hold up against clearly stronger opponents.

Quick stat Your rating, tilted by your score against opponents rated 25+ points above you.
With Deep analysis Your save rate: when Stockfish says you were clearly losing (eval below −1.5), how often you clawed the game back to a draw or win — plus how accurately you play while worse.
♚

Endgame

GRIND

How much better you play long games than short ones.

Quick stat Your rating, tilted by how you score in long games (70+ plies) vs your baseline.
With Deep analysis Your real accuracy in the endgame phase — measured from centipawn loss once the board simplifies (few pieces left), separately from your middlegame. Playing clean endings lifts it directly.
♝

Openings

PREP

Repertoire breadth and how rarely you get blown out early.

How it's computed Two ingredients: how many distinct openings (ECO codes) you play, and how rarely you take a quick loss (under 40 plies). Variety plus not getting steamrolled out of the opening = prepared.

This is a breadth proxy, not a theory-depth measure — a v2 goal is book depth vs a masters database.

♟

Control

COMPOSURE

Composure — how you score in the game right after a loss.

Quick stat Your rating, tilted by how you score in the game right after a loss (tilt resistance).
With Deep analysis Your blunder + mistake rate — the share of your moves where the engine says the position dropped sharply. Fewer big eval swings = more control. This is the most direct read of consistency.

Deep analysis

ENGINE

Real Stockfish, running in your browser.

When you press Deep analysis, a single-threaded build of Stockfish (the same engine that powers top analysis sites) loads in your browser and replays your recent games move by move. For each of your moves it compares the position before and after, and the difference — your centipawn loss — becomes an accuracy score. Accuracy is naturally tied to strength: a world-class player loses ~10–15 centipawns a move, a beginner loses 60–80. That's why the engine version can't be gamed by style alone.

Honest limits It runs on your device, so it analyzes a handful of recent games at moderate depth — enough for a fair read, not a tournament post-mortem. Nothing is uploaded; the analysis happens entirely in your browser. Speed and Openings stay as their quick stats (they don't need an engine).

Stockfish is free software (GPL-3.0); it's bundled with this page, not phoning home.

Card tiers

RARITY

Like ultimate-team cards, your finish is set by strength and story:

BRONZEOverall under 65.
SILVEROverall 65–74.
GOLDOverall 75–89.
PRIMETitled players, or Overall 90+. The premium black-and-gold card.
ICONAccount dormant 1+ years — the retired-great treatment.
LEGENDDormant 5+ years — holographic, the rarest pull.

Rare is the holographic foil finish, layered on top of any tier. You earn it by sitting near the top of your band, having a standout (spiky) stat spread, or being Prime/Icon/Legend.
